Investigation brief & product hypotheses

Why market

Native app subscriptions and a relatively focused business audience make one-board utilities worth testing. A platform pricing advantage does not demonstrate that a new app can acquire paying accounts.

Why now

Native monetization is documented and the platform published a 2025 micro-SaaS portfolio case. The story does not establish first launch dates or marketplace-only customer acquisition.

Recent entrant finding

No complete set of 5–10 successful recent entrants was verified. Launch dates, current traction and payment must be sourced independently; historical success is insufficient.

Promising categories

Board quality; focused reporting — hypotheses, not measured low-competition categories.

Paying categories

Native monetization is a purchasing mechanism. Category-level willingness to pay still needs current paid listing and customer evidence.

Target

Board naming-rule checker

Avoid

Bidirectional multi-board synchronization, calendar scheduling or full CRM

First customer target

Test acquisition of 5 paying customers through marketplace search; this is an experiment target, not a forecast.

Validation

Verify app-definition approval and paid demand for one board workflow; test licensing and account-size pricing.

Stop rule

Do not build before confirming publisher access, a material weakness in direct alternatives, and recent paid customer traction.
